APSCC Contributes to Springer’s Encyclopedia of SDG 15: Life on Land

Dr. Golda A. Edwin and Dr. Nandhivarman Muthu contributed landmark chapters to Springer’s Encyclopedia of the UN SDGs “Life on Land” series — advancing evidence-based land management and soil science globally.

View on Springer ↗

APSCC is proud to have contributed to Life on Land, a volume in Springer’s landmark Encyclopedia of the UN Sustainable Development Goals — a living reference work advancing evidence-based knowledge on land management, soil science and terrestrial ecosystems in support of SDG 15.

Edited under the series leadership of Prof. Walter Leal Filho, the Encyclopedia is a Springer Reference — a continuously updated, peer-reviewed body of scholarship that positions the world’s research community around each of the 17 Sustainable Development Goals. APSCC’s contributions bring soil science and landscape thinking into this global framework.

APSCC’s contributions

Two APSCC scholars shaped entries in the Life on Land series — contributing both authorship and editorial leadership:

Dr. Golda A. EdwinLead Author · Chapter: “Soil Fertility, Integrated Management, and Sustainability” (with Nakkeeran Muthu), Jul 2020
Dr. Nandhivarman MuthuSection Editor · “Landscape” entry

Soil: the skin of the Earth

The chapter “Soil Fertility, Integrated Management, and Sustainability” frames soil as the living foundation of terrestrial life. Drawing on insights from the Soil Science Society of America and the FAO, it makes the case for integrated soil management as a cornerstone of the 2030 Agenda — enhancing land management, supporting resilient food systems and combating land degradation.

Soil is not just dirt — it is the skin of the Earth, the foundation of civilizations and the reservoir of life.

Landscape: a holistic view of sustainability

As Section Editor for the “Landscape” entry, Dr. Nandhivarman Muthu advanced a holistic approach to sustainability — understanding landscapes as living systems shaped by both natural processes and human activity. This framing converges with the UNFCCC’s “Nature for Climate” initiative and the growing global emphasis on nature-based solutions.

Regional relevance, global recognition

India and the wider Asia-Pacific region wrestle with soil degradation, unsustainable agriculture and rapidly changing land use. APSCC’s contributions bring regionally grounded evidence to a global platform — and the Springer Reference series lends international recognition, aligning APSCC’s work firmly with the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development.
